What you are looking at

The same input conditions — wind speed, fuel moisture, fuel bed — pushed through three progressively richer representations of grass-fire spread physics:

Tier 1 is the closed-form regression of Cheney, Gould & Catchpole (1998), Eq. 6, fit to the Australian grass-fire experiments of Cheney, Gould & Catchpole (1993): ROS = ach·U₂0.987·exp(−0.0707·M%). It runs in microseconds — the curve and readout above are computed by JavaScript in this page (View Source to see), and the scatter points are the digitized Fig. 8 experimental data the regression fits. The Marsden-Smedley model (1995) is the equivalent regression for Tasmanian buttongrass moorland, with wind measured at 1.7 m and a fuel build-up factor for stand age.

Tier 2 replaces the regression with a resolved pyrolysis model: a cascade of 1D fuel elements, each running a two-step pyrolysis ROM (kinetics transferred from cone-calorimeter wheat-straw data), heated by radiation from upstream burning cells (Albini line-source view factor with Beer–Lambert blocking) plus wind-driven convective preheating. Each run takes a fraction of a second on the server; results above are precomputed snapshots. The source cell is driven by a sustained 50 kW/m² flux; downstream cells must ignite from spread flux alone.

Tier 3 is the full thing: 3D k-ε RANS with EDC combustion, discrete-ordinates radiation, Lagrangian fuel particles and a level-set front — hours of compute per case, rendered as animations.

Limitations (read these)

  • Tier 1 is a regression over a specific dataset (Annaburroo, NT, Australia): flat ground, cured grass, U10 ≲ 8 m/s. It interpolates well and extrapolates badly.
  • Tier 2 underpredicts field spread rates by one to two orders of magnitude: a single line of 1D elements captures radiative ignition physics but not the convective flame-attachment mechanism that drives real grass-fire propagation. Treat its numbers as mechanism-demonstration, not prediction — that is exactly why Tier 3 (and the empirical forcing used in its low-wind regime) exists.
  • At U10 < ~1.4 m/s fires spread by intermittent flame tongues (Finney et al. 2015), a variance mechanism no mean-field closure represents; production simulators switch to empirical forcing there too.
  • No slope, no spotting, no fire-shape effects, homogeneous fuel only.
